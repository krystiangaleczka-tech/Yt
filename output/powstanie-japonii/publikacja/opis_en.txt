Japan began with gods, monsters, and four brothers who set out to rule it. Only one survived. This is the entire founding myth of Japan, from a drop of salt on a spear to the first emperor's throne in 660 BC, told in paper theatre.

The gods Izanagi and Izanami stir the first island out of the sea. The sun goddess Amaterasu hides in a cave and the world goes dark. Her brother Susanoo, god of storms, gets the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi drunk on sake and finds a sword in its tail: the Kusanagi, one of the three sacred treasures of Japan. Then Prince Iwarebiko, the future Emperor Jimmu, sails east with his brothers, loses all three of them, follows the crow Yatagarasu through the mountains of Kumano and wins his last battle under a golden kite. Finally: what the chronicles, archaeology and a gold seal found by a farmer say about whether Jimmu ever existed, and why the sword is still wrapped in cloth that no one opens.
