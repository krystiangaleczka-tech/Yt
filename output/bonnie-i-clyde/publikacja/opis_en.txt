Bonnie Parker didn't smoke cigars. One joke photo said she did, and the newspapers turned two broke kids from Dallas into the most famous outlaw couple in America. This is the entire Bonnie and Clyde story, from the slums of West Dallas to the ambush on a Louisiana road in 1934, told in paper theatre.

Clyde Barrow grows up in the Devil's Back Porch, meets a waitress who writes poems, breaks out of jail with the pistol she smuggles in, and swears he will never go back after the Eastham prison farm. A camera left behind in Joplin, Missouri, puts their faces on every front page. Then the glamour runs out: the burning car at Wellington, the shootouts at Platte City and Dexfield Park, the killings at Grapevine that turn America against them. And one patient man, retired Texas Ranger Frank Hamer, stops chasing them, reads their file instead, and waits 102 days for one mistake. Finally: who owns the legend, from the souvenir hunters and the 1967 film to a video game in 2026.
