Narracja:
Two stories. One scientist. Nine months apart. Ice, or something alive. The only way to know was to get closer. So NOAA did. Researchers placed hydrophones nearer to Antarctica, and in 2005 they had the answer. The Bloop was an icequake: an iceberg cracking and breaking away from an Antarctic glacier. Three years later, the same kind of listening followed an iceberg called A53a as it broke apart near South Georgia Island. This is what that sounded like.
